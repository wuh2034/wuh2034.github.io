A data flywheel for video world models: the VLM decides, the world model generates, the result is judged, and what survives comes back as training data.

The problem

Autoregressive video world models drift. Over a long rollout the target drops out of frame, the scene state slides away from what was asked for, and responses to action commands become unreliable. This thesis treats those failures as a data problem: if a vision-language model can watch a rollout chunk by chunk and decide what should happen next, the same loop that generates video can also generate the supervision needed to fix it.

Interaction data engine

A fully automatic loop — video generation → VLM understanding → action planning → next-chunk generation — run at chunk granularity so a decision takes effect immediately rather than at the end of a rollout. It has accumulated roughly 200 scenes and 12,000 trajectories; each trajectory is 57 frames across 5 chunks (3.56 s), for about 60,000 chunks and 12 hours of video.

Data governance

  • Two-stage quality control combining rule-based filters with VLM semantic evaluation, covering decode stability, target visibility, action outcome, temporal consistency and generation artefacts.
  • Every clip carries an aligned prompt, camera-control signal and quality label, so the dataset is directly usable as conditioned supervision.
  • Train and test are split by scene, which keeps near-duplicate chunks from the same rollout out of both sides and avoids leakage.

World-model fine-tuning

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning of LingBot-World v2 14B causal-fast: the VAE and UMT5 encoders stay frozen, Rank-32 LoRA is applied to the DiT attention Q/K/V/Out projections, and the camera-projection and AdaLN control modules are trained. BF16, gradient checkpointing and precomputed latent/text features keep multi-GPU H100 training inside its memory budget.

Inference runtime

LingBot-World v2 and Qwen3-VL are served persistently through SGLang, with LockStep scheduling so the VLM's feedback lands in the very next chunk instead of a chunk later. FlashAttention together with KV-cache window tuning brought 12-frame generation latency down from 4.49 s to 2.47 s (−45%) and cut peak VRAM by 7.1 GiB (−8.4%) — the difference between a loop you can iterate on and one you cannot.
