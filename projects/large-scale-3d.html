The problem

City-scale capture — buildings, blocks, campuses — breaks feed-forward reconstruction in three ways at once: a long video does not fit into one forward pass, every chunk comes back in its own local coordinate frame and scale, and error accumulates across chunks. The pipeline is built around exactly those three failures: video chunking → local reconstruction → cross-batch alignment → scale recovery → dense representation.

Fixed-budget chunking

Roughly 1,300 self-captured frames are uniformly sampled and screened for motion blur with a Laplacian criterion, then organised into fixed 40-frame batches with deliberate overlap. The fixed budget bounds compute and VRAM per step; the overlap is what later gives cross-batch alignment stable correspondences to work with.

Local geometry recovery

VGGT-1B runs per batch, predicting camera parameters, depth and confidence, which are back-projected into a dense point cloud. Confidence filtering does real work here — it is what stops the building's glass facade and other reflective surfaces from poisoning the fusion with bad depth.

Metric scale estimation

Each batch is split into three interleaved subsequences, and MASt3R's metric point cloud is used to match scale and translation between them. The resulting scale estimates are screened by cross-subsequence variance, keeping only the stable ones — that screening is what turns a set of locally correct reconstructions into a single metrically consistent scene.

Dense representation and results

NKSR recovers dense surfaces from the fused cloud and CityGaussian handles large-scale Gaussian rendering. As a team we ran the full path from video to point cloud, mesh and Gaussian representation, on the TUM building's long trajectory as well as on city-scale datasets.
